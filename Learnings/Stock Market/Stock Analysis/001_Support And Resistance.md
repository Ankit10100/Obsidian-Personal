
## Support:

* Floor, usually if stock prices touches this number lots of buyers kicks in and doesn't let the price fall further.
  Price falling → reaches support → demand increases → price may rise.
	* **Why it works:**
		- Buyers think the stock is cheap at that level.
		- Existing shareholders buy more.
		- Short sellers may close their positions.
		- 


## Resistance The "Ceiling" :

* A **resistance level** is a price area where sellers are strong enough to stop the stock from rising further.
* Buyers dominate, they sell and price will not rise further.
* 📈 Price rising → reaches resistance → supply increases → price may fall.


The stock may bounce between support and resistance many times.


## How to Identify Support and Resistance:

* If the price has bounced several times from a level, it may be support.
* Similarly, repeated failures near a higher level indicate resistance.


### Watch Volume at These Levels

A support or resistance level becomes more important when:

- Price touches it multiple times.
- Trading volume increases near the level.
- The stock reacts strongly afterward.


### Role Reversal

One of the most useful concepts:

- Old resistance often becomes new support.
- Old support often becomes new resistance.


### Understand the Possible Scenarios

#### Scenario A: Support Holds:
Resistance: 520; Support: 495

Microsoft drops to $493 and then rebounds.

Many traders would view this as a bullish sign because buyers defended support.

#### Scenario B: Resistance Breaks

If Microsoft closes convincingly above $520 with strong volume, many traders expect further upside because the old ceiling has been removed

#### Scenario C: Support Breaks

If Microsoft falls below $490-$495 and stays there, some traders expect further weakness because buyers failed to defend support.


### Quick Cheat Sheet

This for candles in chart.

|Candle|Meaning|
|---|---|
|🟢 Green|Close > Open (buyers won)|
|🔴 Red|Close < Open (sellers won)|
|Long Green Body|Strong buying|
|Long Red Body|Strong selling|
|Long Lower Wick|Buyers defended price (support)|
|Long Upper Wick|Sellers rejected higher prices (resistance)|


Q) So to form support and resistance channel should I look at wick or open close?
A) Use both, but prioritize the candle bodies (open/close) and treat the wicks as the edges of the zone.
### Why?

The **open and close** represent where the market spent most of its time and ultimately agreed on a price.
The **wicks** show where the market briefly explored before being rejected.
For support and resistance, you're trying to find **areas where buyers or sellers consistently stepped in**, not necessarily the exact highest or lowest tick.
